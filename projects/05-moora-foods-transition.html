The problem

Moora Foods ran three Subway franchise outlets in Dubai, with over 70 staff and a decade or more of customer data. The business was being sold.

Handing it over meant moving assets, leases, staff, systems and sensitive paperwork between two parties while the shops stayed open.

My role

I was the core strategic lead for the sale. I mapped equipment, leases and asset valuations, built the profit and loss breakdowns, prepared the pricing feasibility for the buyers (KOJ) and handled the sensitive documents: visas, passports, serial numbers and bank accounts.

I also ran the retraining. I was the person holding eight relationships steady at once.

Stakeholder Map

One person, eight relationships to hold steady at once.

Key decisions

01

I moved store by store.

It was the safest way to move stock and keep the shops trading. One store at a time meant a problem stayed in one place.

02

I phased the Oracle training.

I ran it with Oracle courses, in six steps: briefing, store setup, hands-on training, live testing, a knowledge check, then go-live with support.

03

I used the numbers to hold the pricing conversation.

The hardest part was the constant back and forth with the buyers over price. I walked them through the profit and loss to show the potential and value of each store.

04

I agreed terms between both parties first.

That let trading carry on without a single day of disruption.

Reflection

A sale looks like paperwork, and it is really about people. Many livelihoods sat inside this handover, so I treated every decision, from pricing to training, as something that lands on someone. It is the project that taught me to weigh the human cost next to the commercial one.
